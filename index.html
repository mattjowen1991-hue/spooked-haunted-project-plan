Editing in CapCut

Why it's taking days

The big idea

Most of the time goes on watching everything, start to finish, on every camera. A three-camera night is many hours of footage, and you can't edit faster than you can watch. The trick is to stop watching it all, find the moments first, then cut everything in between.

The slow way
  1. Load everything
  2. Watch it all from the start
  3. Trim as you go
  4. Lose track, start again
The quick way
  1. Line the three cameras up once
  2. Jump straight to the moments
  3. Keep those, bin the rest
  4. Arrange them into the episode
Step by step

Edit an investigation

1

Get the files in order

Before you open CapCut

About 10 minutes
  1. Make one folder for the investigation, with a folder inside for each camera: Canon A, Canon B, GoPro, Drone.
  2. Copy each memory card into its own folder. Don't mix cameras, because the Canons name their files in the same way.
  3. Keep everything on one drive, ideally a fast external SSD or your computer's main drive. Editing straight off a memory card or a slow USB stick is painfully slow.
2

Start the project

And stop CapCut lagging

About 5 minutes
  1. Open CapCut and click Create project.
  2. Drag the camera folders into the media panel at the top left.
  3. Turn on proxies. GoPro and Canon files are big, and big files make the timeline stutter. In the project settings, find Performance and switch on Proxy at 720p. CapCut makes small copies to edit with, but still exports from the full-quality originals. On some versions the settings are behind a Modify button at the top right.
  4. Set the project to 1080p and the same frame rate as the Canons.

This one setting is often the difference between CapCut being smooth and CapCut being unusable with long footage.

3

Line up the three cameras

One vigil at a time

About 5 minutes a vigil

This is why the team claps at the start of every vigil. On the timeline, the clap shows up as a sharp spike in each camera's sound wave, so you can line them up by eye.

The simple way, works on the free version

  1. Put the GoPro clip on the bottom track. It's the wide shot that covers everything, so it's your safety net.
  2. Put Canon A on the track above it, and Canon B above that.
  3. Zoom into the timeline (Ctrl +, or Cmd + on a Mac) and drag each clip left or right until the clap spikes sit exactly on top of each other.
  4. Mute all but the camera with the best sound, usually the Canon closest to whoever's talking. Otherwise you'll hear an echo.

To switch angles, cut the top clips and delete the bits you don't want. Whatever's underneath shows through. So deleting a section of Canon B shows Canon A, and deleting both shows the GoPro.

The quicker way, if your version has it

CapCut also has a multi-camera tool. Select the three clips, right-click, choose Create multi-camera clip and pick Audio to sync. You then click between angles in the preview while it plays. If it shows a Pro badge, use the simple way above instead. It does the same job.

4

Find the moments

The step that saves the most time

About 30 minutes for the whole night

Don't watch it all. Find the good bits first, then drop a marker on each one so you can jump back to it.

  1. Use the log. If the team used the SHP Log on the night, the times of every knock, voice and reaction are already written down. The Combine tab even says how far into each vigil it happened, counted from the clap.
  2. Read the sound wave. Bangs, shouts, voices and laughter show up as spikes. Long flat stretches are usually people standing in silence, which is exactly what gets cut.
  3. Listen for the call-outs. When someone says "did you hear that?", the moment is just before it.
  4. Mark it. Put the playhead on the moment and press M to drop a marker.
5

Cut out the waiting

Keep the moments, bin the rest

About 1 to 2 hours

Two keys do most of the work, and they close the gap for you automatically:

  • Q deletes everything to the left of the playhead
  • W deletes everything to the right of the playhead

To cut a boring stretch out of the middle, put the playhead where it starts and press Ctrl B (Cmd B on a Mac) to split. Do the same where it ends, click the piece in between and press Delete.

What to keep

  • Anything that happened, plus a few seconds before it and the reaction after
  • The questions that got a response
  • Genuine reactions and funny moments
  • The bits where you debunk something. They make the real moments more believable.

What to cut

  • Walking between rooms, setting up and packing down
  • Questions that got nothing back. Keep one or two so viewers feel the wait, cut the rest.
  • Long silences. A few seconds builds tension, a minute loses viewers.

Rule of thumb: if you wouldn't call someone over to watch it, cut it.

6

Put the episode in order

The shape the big shows use

About 1 hour

Once the vigils are trimmed, drag the pieces into this order. Aim for 35 to 50 minutes in total.

0:00
Cold open

The best moment of the night, before anything else. This is what keeps people watching.

0:45
Title

Your intro, short. Save it once and reuse it every episode.

1:00
The location

Drone shots and the history of the place.

4:00
The team and the plan

Who's there and what you're hoping to find.

6:00
The vigils

Room by room, in the order you did them.

35:00
Evidence review

Replay the best moments. See step 7.

42:00
Verdict

What you made of the night, a question for the comments, and where you're going next.

7

Make the evidence clear

So viewers hear what you heard

About 30 minutes
  1. Play it twice. Copy the moment and play it again straight after. First as it happened, then the replay.
  2. Turn it up. Select the clip, open the audio settings on the right and raise the volume for the replay.
  3. Caption it. Add a text box saying what you think was said or heard, like "Get out?". Add it by hand with Text, so it works on the free version.
  4. Freeze or zoom. For something seen, use the Freeze button above the timeline to hold the frame, and zoom in on the spot.
  5. Be honest. Label the replay as enhanced, and don't add effects that weren't there. Viewers trust a team that shows its working.
8

Export

Ready for YouTube

Set it running, then make a cuppa
  1. Click Export at the top right.
  2. Resolution 1080p, frame rate to match your project, format MP4, codec H.264.
  3. Pick the higher quality bitrate option if there's a choice.
  4. Before you upload, write down the time each room starts. Paste them into the YouTube description as chapters, like 12:40 The cellar, so viewers can jump around.

If 4K export or anything else shows a lock or Pro badge, it'll ask you to pay or add a watermark. 1080p looks great on YouTube and keeps you on the free version.

9

Make the Shorts

From the same edit

About 10 minutes each
  1. Go back to the CapCut home screen, find the project and duplicate it.
  2. Open the copy and change the ratio to 9:16 (portrait).
  3. Keep one moment, 20 to 60 seconds long, and delete everything else.
  4. Zoom each clip in so the person or the spot fills the frame. If they move around, use keyframes to follow them.
  5. Add a caption at the top saying what happened, then export the same way.

CapCut's automatic reframe does the zooming for you, but it's now a paid feature. Doing it by hand only takes a few minutes.

Free or paid

You can do all of this for free

CapCut moved some features behind its paid plan in 2025, and its own pages don't fully agree on which ones. The easy rule: if a button has a Pro badge or a lock on it, it'll ask you to pay or add a watermark when you export. Everything in the steps above has a free way of doing it.

Cutting, splitting, text, keyframes, freeze frameFree
1080p exportFree
Multi-camera toolCheckStack the cameras on tracks (step 3)
Auto captionsCheckType captions by hand for the key moments. YouTube also adds automatic subtitles for free.
Reduce noisePaidTurn the replay up and caption it instead
Auto reframePaidZoom by hand (step 9)

If you ever do want the paid version, CapCut Pro was reported at £21.99 a month in the UK in 2026. You don't need it for any of this.

One thing to know: CapCut's terms give it wide rights over content uploaded to its servers. If that bothers you, save projects on your computer rather than in CapCut's cloud space.

The first one

Go easy on the first episode

  • The first edit is always the slowest. Steps 3 to 5 get much quicker once you've done them a couple of times.
  • Save your intro, outro and title text the first time, and reuse them every episode.
  • Save often. CapCut autosaves, but it's worth pressing Ctrl S anyway.
  • Done is better than perfect. A tight 40 minutes beats a perfect 90.
